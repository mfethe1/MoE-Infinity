## Question

Can the single atomicAdd site in `grouped_moe_decode_pro6000.cu` (down-projection
epilogue) be replaced by plain stores + a fixed-order combine kernel to make grouped
decode bit-reproducible across processes at acceptable cost?

## Design (both attempts)

- Params gains `det_scratch` fp32 + `det_mode` (env `MOE_GROUPED_DETERMINISTIC=1`); under
  det the epilogue plain-stores `o * s_w[i]` per (token, key, h) instead of atomicAdd;
  a `det_combine_kernel` (256 threads) sums in fixed key order into `out`; per-step
  `cudaMemsetAsync` zeroes the scratch; `zero_out` pass skipped.
- Attempt-1 key: run-varying rank slot `(t, tok>>16)`. Attempt-2 key: canonical expert id
  `(t, e)`, host-computed `e_cap = max(expert_id)+1` via `expert_table.to(torch::kCPU)`
  (deref of the CUDA tensor on host segfaults — fixed in build9).

## Conclusion

**Epilogue accumulation order is not the determinism lever.** Two independent fixed-order
designs — rank-keyed and canonical expert-id-keyed — both leave decode diverging across
fresh processes, and both are *less* reproducible than the atomic baseline (7/8). The
nondeterminism therefore enters upstream of the MoE down-projection (router gate GEMM,
attention, or per-expert input values differ run-to-run); by the time values reach the
epilogue, identical summation order cannot recover bit-reproducibility. A det epilogue
also costs ~5–38% decode throughput depending on contention. Follow-on scope (det router
+ det prefill/attention) is a different, larger experiment class; the speed frontier
(sm_120-tuned kernel instantiations) is the better next lever. Priors updated in
`STACK-1001-79990/BELIEFS.json`.
